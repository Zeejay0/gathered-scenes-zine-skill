# Adaptive Narrative Flat Tear Path v0.2

Choose the tear path from the finished Gathered Scenes Zine poster, not from the raw photograph alone. The final poster reveals the actual photo anchor, paper field, photo-to-illustration passage, source-color illustration, high-saturation guide, text, focal subject, and quiet entry zones that the animation must respect.

## Composition order

1. Identify the main photographic anchor and the primary photo-to-illustration passage.
2. Identify the dominant visual gesture and narrative order: what establishes the environment, what relationship appears next, where the focal subject arrives, and where the eye rests.
3. Find a quiet canvas edge or paper region that does not contain text, a face, or the core subject. Prefer it as the crack origin.
4. Compose a path that makes the photographic anchor emerge progressively and places the main subject in the middle third of the reveal, not at the first instant.
5. Give the reveal a narrative rhythm: quiet introduction, directional progression, focal reveal, then visual release or balance.
6. Relate the path to the high-saturation guide without tracing it. The static guide organizes attention; the tear path organizes time.
7. Use one custom 3–7 point path. Do not choose from preset directions.

## Path rules

- Begin near a quiet canvas edge and end near another edge or calm release zone.
- Do not use top-to-bottom or bottom-to-top paths.
- Do not reduce the route to one rigid straight horizontal or diagonal axis.
- Use changes of height and direction only when they serve a ridge, shoreline, route, gaze, subject relationship, or visual-weight transition.
- Let one or two broader bends create narrative movement; avoid periodic waves, digital zigzags, loops, and arbitrary detours.
- Avoid cutting directly through faces, bodies, text, or defining objects. Approach, pass beside, or turn around them when that strengthens the reveal.
- Reveal the primary subject during the middle third, then resolve toward a secondary focal area, warm light, open space, or balancing mass.

## Custom path

Pass normalized points with `--direction custom --path-points "x,y;x,y;..."`. Use 3–7 points and keep the route coherent enough to read as one tear. Approximate the compositional story rather than tracing every notch because the renderer adds its own broad, high-amplitude low-frequency irregularity and fiber variation.

## Tie-breakers

- Choose the path that starts in more negative space.
- Prefer the direction with the clearest sequence of context → relationship → focal subject → resting point.
- Reveal text late enough to remain a quiet resting point.
- Avoid beginning directly on a face, body, animal, or defining object.
- When two paths are equally plausible, choose the one whose motion agrees with the source gesture and reveals the primary subject later.
- If semantic judgment remains uncertain, use a shallow three-point lateral curve through quiet paper; never fall back to a named preset or vertical reveal.

The poster remains frozen for every path. Only the cover sheet, procedural tear mask, and fiber fringe move.
